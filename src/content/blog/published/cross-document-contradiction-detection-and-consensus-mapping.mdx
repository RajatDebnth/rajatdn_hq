When software engineers build simple Q&A bots, the underlying assumption is that documents agree: the knowledge base holds a single objective ground truth, and the model's job is to locate and summarize it.

In real-world scientific research, clinical medicine, investment analysis, and legal research, **documents actively disagree with one another**.
- Two clinical trials evaluate the same pharmaceutical compound and reach opposite conclusions regarding patient efficacy due to differing cohort sizes.
- Two equity research analysts evaluate the same enterprise software stock and issue conflicting price targets based on divergent revenue growth assumptions.
- Two legal contracts or judicial precedents interpret an indemnification statute in conflicting ways.

If your AI research assistant naively mashes these retrieved documents together into a single unified summary, it produces an incoherent, hallucinatory compromise: it either declares one paper's finding as absolute fact while ignoring the other, or synthesizes a muddled average that misleads the researcher.

To build an authoritative AI research assistant, engineering teams must implement **Automated Cross-Document Contradiction Detection and Consensus Mapping**.

---

### The Architecture of Consensus and Contradiction Mapping

Instead of a single-pass summary, the synthesis engine analyzes retrieved evidence through a structured comparative matrix:

```
[Retrieved Document Context Across 4 Studies]
               |
               v
[Stage 1: Atomic Claim Extraction (Structured Output)]
Extracts core claims, study sample sizes (N), methodology, and outcomes:
- Study A (2023): Outcome = +14% Improvement (N = 1,200, Double-blind)
- Study B (2024): Outcome = No Statistical Significance (N = 85, Observational)
- Study C (2024): Outcome = +11% Improvement (N = 450, Double-blind)
               |
               v
[Stage 2: Cross-Claim Pairwise Stance Classification]
Evaluates relationships between pairs of extracted claims:
- Relationship(Study A, Study C) ===> CONSENSUS (Strong agreement)
- Relationship(Study A, Study B) ===> CONTRADICTION (Conflicting outcomes)
               |
               v
[Stage 3: Evidence-Weighted Synthesis & Discrepancy Attribution]
Generates comparative analysis explaining WHY the studies diverge:
"While Study A and C report statistically significant efficacy in double-blind trials,
Study B observed no significance, likely attributable to its smaller observational cohort."
```

### Step-by-Step Implementation with Pydantic

#### 1. Structured Claim and Methodology Extraction

Before comparing documents, extract structured claims along with key methodological attributes:

```python
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field
from typing import List, Literal

class ExtractedClaim(BaseModel):
    source_doc_id: str
    finding_summary: str = Field(description="One-sentence factual assertion")
    sentiment: Literal["positive", "negative", "inconclusive"]
    sample_size: int = Field(description="Number of participants or sample count")
    methodology_type: str = Field(description="e.g., Randomized Controlled Trial, Observational")
    confidence_interval: str = Field(default="N/A")

class PaperClaimExtraction(BaseModel):
    claims: List[ExtractedClaim]
```

#### 2. Pairwise Contradiction and Stance Detection

Evaluate the logical relationship between claims using a specialized prompt with constrained classification:

```python
class ClaimComparison(BaseModel):
    claim_a_id: str
    claim_b_id: str
    relationship: Literal["consensus", "contradiction", "orthogonal"]
    conflict_driver: Literal["sample_size", "differing_timeframe", "methodology_divergence", "none"]
    explanation: str

async def compare_claims(claim_a: ExtractedClaim, claim_b: ExtractedClaim) -> ClaimComparison:
    completion = client.beta.chat.completions.parse(
        model="gpt-4o",
        messages=[
            {
                "role": "system", 
                "content": "You are a scientific peer reviewer. Compare two extracted findings for direct agreement, conflict, or independence."
            },
            {
                "role": "user", 
                "content": f"Claim A: {claim_a.model_dump_json()}\nClaim B: {claim_b.model_dump_json()}"
            }
        ],
        response_format=ClaimComparison
    )
    return completion.choices[0].message.parsed
```

#### 3. Evidence-Weighted Consensus Mapping in the UI

In the frontend application, present findings using structured visual cards rather than plain text paragraphs:

```
[Synthesis View: Efficacy of Treatment X]
Consensus Score: 75% Agreement across 4 reviewed studies

Consensus Findings (3 Studies):
- Significant reduction in symptom duration (Avg: -2.4 days)
- Supported by Study 1 (N=1,200), Study 3 (N=450), Study 4 (N=310)

Divergent / Contradictory Findings (1 Study):
- Study 2 (N=85) reported no statistically significant variance.
- Key Methodological Difference: Retrospective observational cohort vs. randomized double-blind.
```

This presentation provides researchers with the nuanced perspective they need to evaluate the scientific validity of the literature.

### Key Takeaways for Technical Founders

- **Never Average Contradictory Claims:** An AI assistant that tells a doctor `"The drug reduces risk by 7%"` when one study says 14% and another says 0% is generating dangerous, fabricated compromises.
- **Surface Methodology Differences:** Real experts evaluate findings based on sample size, study design, and publication dates; your extraction pipeline must capture these metadata parameters.
- **Turn Contradictions into Interactive Filter Controls:** Allow researchers to toggle filters (e.g., `"Show only Double-Blind Trials with N > 500"`) to recalculate consensus dynamically.
