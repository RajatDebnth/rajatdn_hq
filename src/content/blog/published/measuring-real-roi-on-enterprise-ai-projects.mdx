Over 80% of enterprise generative AI pilots stall in proof-of-concept purgatory. Executives fund exciting hackathon prototypes, celebrate initial demos, but quietly abandon the initiatives six months later because nobody can demonstrate a measurable financial return on investment (ROI). By reading this guide, you will understand how to transition from vanity metrics to concrete unit economics and design workflows that deliver undeniable enterprise value.

## What is it?

Measuring real ROI on enterprise AI is a financial and operational framework that ties every model transaction directly to business KPIs:

1. **Cost per Resolved Unit (CPRU)**: Measures the total operational cost (model tokens, vector compute, infrastructure) required to complete a single business task compared to the manual baseline cost.
2. **Cycle Time Compression**: Measures the end-to-end reduction in time required to complete high-value operational processes (such as contract review or customer onboarding).
3. **Workflow Redesign**: Rather than attempting to automate 100% of an employee's job, the system automates specific bottleneck sub-tasks and hands clean structured drafts to human experts.

## Why does it matter?

Relying on qualitative excitement rather than hard financial metrics leads to inevitable project cancellation:

- **The Demo Trap**: Proving that an LLM can summarize a meeting transcript is technically simple, but if employees spend the same amount of time reviewing and editing the summary, net productivity gains equal zero.
- **Uncontrolled Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)**: Failing to factor in vector database hosting, prompt evaluation tooling, and engineering maintenance costs leads to unexpected budget overruns.
- **Workflow Mismatch**: Injecting an AI assistant as a separate browser tab creates context-switching friction; unless the tool is embedded directly into the employee's existing daily software, adoption collapses.

Tracking unit economics proves clear value to executive sponsors and secures long-term operational budget.

## How does it work?

A production ROI measurement model calculates direct economic value using three concrete equations:

### 1. Cost per Resolved Task (CPRU) Equation
Calculate the net financial savings per business task:

> **Net Savings per Task** = Manual Labor Cost − (AI Token Cost + Compute Overhead + Human Review Cost)

```python
def calculate_task_unit_economics(manual_hours: float, hourly_wage_usd: float, token_cost_usd: float, review_hours: float = 0.05) -> dict:
    manual_cost = manual_hours * hourly_wage_usd
    ai_execution_cost = token_cost_usd + (review_hours * hourly_wage_usd)
    net_savings = manual_cost - ai_execution_cost
    roi_percentage = (net_savings / ai_execution_cost) * 100 if ai_execution_cost > 0 else 0
    
    return {
        "manual_cost_usd": round(manual_cost, 2),
        "ai_total_cost_usd": round(ai_execution_cost, 2),
        "net_savings_usd": round(net_savings, 2),
        "roi_percentage": round(roi_percentage, 1)
    }
```

### 2. High-Friction Task Identification
Focus AI automation strictly on high-volume, low-variability operational bottlenecks:
- Parsing multi-vendor utility bills into standardized ERP entries.
- Summarizing 40-page technical discovery calls into structured Jira tickets.
- Triaging Tier-1 IT support tickets with grounded knowledge base articles.

### 3. Closed-Loop Feedback Logging
Every automated task must emit an event log recording execution duration, user acceptance rate (whether the employee accepted the AI draft without major edits), and downstream error rates to measure sustained quality over time.
